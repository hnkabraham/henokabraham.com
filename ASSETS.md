# Scene assets

## Current airborne Dreamliner showcase

- `public/models/dreamliner-787-9.glb` (1,308,580 bytes Draco-compressed, 105,359 triangles, ten material groups) derives from [FlightGear 787-family, commit 02626b5](https://github.com/IskenderWang/787-family/tree/02626b5b659d89b9399943ee167bbb0b46f0c871): `Models/787-9.ac`, `Models/GEnx.ac`, the matching 787-9 white paint map, GEnx texture and wing texture. **GPL-2.0**, authors as listed in upstream `Authors.md`; acknowledgements include Joshua W, Omega95, MSA-S23, Jonathan Redpath, Pilot2938 and the current maintainers.
- `scripts/prepare-dreamliner.py --cache /path/to/source` fetches the pinned source if absent, preserves metre coordinates and AC3D crease normals/UVs, triangulates polygons, merges by material, removes deployed landing gear, duplicate fan blur discs and invisible compressor stacks, and encodes textures to WebP (maximum 4096²). The main fuselage map is resampled from its 8192² source. Both inlet fan assemblies retain 10,360 triangles each. Two fans rotate in the runtime; fuselage/wing finishes receive elastic flex and a fictional Henok Abraham livery. No flight-simulator code is executed.
- `scripts/compress-dreamliner.mjs` applies Draco (edgebreaker; 16-bit positions, 10-bit normals, 12-bit UVs) to that output, taking it from 5,272,964 to 1,308,580 bytes; the encoder drops degenerate faces, 105,622 to 105,359 triangles. The decoder in `public/draco/` is three.js's copy of Google Draco (Apache-2.0, credited in `public/credits/draco.txt`) and is served beside the model under the same content version.
- `public/models/dreamliner-787-9-phone.glb` (611,036 bytes) is built from the compressed model by `scripts/prepare-dreamliner-phone.py`: the Draco buffers are copied unchanged and the three 4096² WebP maps are resampled to 2048² (Lanczos, WebP quality 88), 888,538 bytes of textures becoming 190,988. Viewports narrower than 800 px fetch it in place of the wide model; both are versioned and deployed together.
- `public/credits/dreamliner-source.zip` (9,919,754 bytes) contains the corresponding .blend/.ac editable source, original textures, author list, GPL-2.0 license and conversion script. `/credits/dreamliner.html` links it visibly from the active scene and footer; the aircraft derivative remains GPL-2.0.
- The existing generated `cruise-sky.jpg` and `cloud-sprite.png` remain the background for the whole tour. Compositor animations pause offscreen and for reduced motion. The existing CC0 `scenery/daylight.hdr` supplies aircraft reflections. The active experience loads no terrain tiles or mapping service.

## Garage: 2016 Shelby GT350R

- Source: [2016 Ford Mustang Shelby GT350R](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/2016-ford-mustang-shelby-gt350r-1d03bb121b6e4371b0978dc584b788d8) by [Ddiaz Design](https://sketchfab.com/ddiaz-design), Sketchfab, [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). This is a stock download, not a scan of my own car; the source USDZ is not committed to this repository (see `.gitignore`) since there's no reason to carry a 4 MB zip in history when the derivative is what ships, and the licence doesn't require withholding the source anyway.
- `scripts/prepare-garage-car.py <path-to-usdz>` reads the USDZ (via `usdcat`, converting its binary `scene.usdc` to text), merges the 550 separate mesh prims into one primitive per material (19 materials, plus two geometric splits below), composes each mesh's world transform (row-vector convention; the file's own `xformOp:scale(100,100,100)` cancels the stage's `metersPerUnit = 0.01`, verified against the real car's published dimensions rather than assumed), and hand-writes a GLB in the same JSON+BIN pattern as `scripts/prepare-dreamliner.py`. `node scripts/compress-dreamliner.mjs <raw.glb> public/models/garage-gt350r.glb` (used unmodified) does the Draco pass. Local: `public/models/garage-gt350r.glb`, 886,116 bytes Draco-compressed from a raw 4,972,404 (126,246 triangles, 21 primitives, no geometry pruned). The GLB embeds its author, licence and source URL in both `asset.copyright` and a top-level `extras` object, matching the aircraft GLB's convention; the derivative remains CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, credited at `/credits/garage.html`.
- The body paint is a 256² flat-color swatch, not a photo: a blue field with two black racing-stripe bands each edged by a thin red pinstripe. Recolored by nearest-reference-color classification: the field to a body gray, the bands to a stripe blue, and — matching a crisp dark edge visible in Henok's own photos of the car, rather than blending it into the stripe — the pinstripe to the same near-black used for the carbon trim below. Colors are photo-sampled from two photos Henok sent of his actual car (clean, glare-free patches of quarter-panel paint and the front bumper stripe), not guessed: body gray `#5f656b`, stripe blue `#092e70`, carbon/trim dark `#1a1a1c`.
- A second small flat-color swatch (used for various trim, "Coloured" in the source materials) turned out to sample only three colors across everything it's ever used for: a light-gray cell covering the front splitter, rocker/side skirts, rear wing and exhaust surround — rendering them plain white/light instead of the real car's black aero pieces — a tiny gold-brown cell that turned out to be a thin windshield-washer-nozzle wire on the hood (the source of what first looked like a stray rendering artifact), and a dark-navy cell (window seals) that was already correct and is left untouched. The first two are recolored to the same carbon-dark.
- The exhaust tips sample that identical light-gray cell, so recoloring it would have turned them black too — wrong, real tips are metallic light gray. Since a texture recolor can't give one shared pixel two different output colors depending on which mesh samples it, the tips (1,824 triangles, found by position clustering and confirmed against a silhouette render) are split into their own `ExhaustTip_chrome` primitive with a flat metallic finish instead.
- The mirror caps are part of the body-paint mesh, and by coincidence of their UV unwrap land inside the same V-band the hood/roof stripe uses — rendering them stripe-blue rather than black. Not spatially separable from the body (the mesh is topologically continuous with the fender, no 3D gap to bound), so they're isolated by a combined filter instead — outboard position _and_ stripe-band UV, verified to isolate exactly two clean, symmetric clusters (404 triangles) — and split into their own flat-black `MirrorCap_black` primitive.
- Interior (42,851 triangles) and engine bay (13,625 triangles) are the two largest remaining chunks and the first candidates to prune if the Garage viewer stays exterior-only and wants a smaller download; nothing has been pruned so far.
- `public/images/garage-gt350r.png`/`.avif`: the poster shown before the interactive viewer loads (and for reduced-motion visitors), cropped from a verification render of the finished GLB.

## Daylight

- [Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky)](https://polyhaven.com/a/kloofendal_48d_partly_cloudy_puresky), Greg Zaal and Jarod Guest, [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license).
- Local: `public/scenery/daylight.hdr`, 512 × 256, 380,181 bytes, halved from the 1024 × 512 original by `scripts/prepare-daylight-hdr.py` (box-averaged in linear light; it only feeds a prefiltered reflection map). Image-based illumination and reflections for the fallback sky only; it is not requested when the atmosphere renders. Under Takram's scattering renderer the sun colour comes from the transmittance table and image-based light from a sky cubemap rendered around the aircraft, so the visible sky, the haze and the lit surfaces share one set of units. Three.js's Sky shader remains the compatibility fallback.

## Clouds and fallback

- `public/images/cloud-sprite.png`, 1024px transparent generated cumulus. It drifts across the opening sky; browsers with AVIF receive `cloud-sprite.avif` (40,267 bytes, quality 72, encoded from the PNG by Pillow) through `image-set()`.
- Generation prompt: Photoreal isolated white cumulus cloud cluster, landscape 3:2, transparent RGBA background, luminous upper-right sunlight, cool pale-blue shadows, feathered semitransparent wisps, generous transparent margins. No sky, floor, horizon, aircraft, text, or logos.
- `public/images/golden-gate.avif` and `.png` (373×892): the Golden Gate Bridge seen down its span above a fog line, rendered from my own `archive/models/golden-gate-bridge.usdz` (3.75 M triangles; only the 522 meshes rising above the fog floor are loaded) with `scripts/prepare-golden-gate.swift`, `scripts/render-golden-gate.mjs` and `scripts/make-golden-gate-sprite.py`. The model's own materials carry no colour, so the render paints it International Orange (#c0362c) under the sky's warm upper-right light; a second pass records world height and camera distance per pixel, from which the fog hides everything below 60 m above the deck and the haze thickens from 20% at the near tower to 55% at the far one. The model came from a download of mine with no source or licence attached, so it is not committed to this repository (only the rendered sprite is); reproducing this step needs your own USDZ at that path.
- `public/images/golden-gate-portrait.avif` and `.png` (518×669): the same bridge for portrait phones, where the cover crop shows only the photo's middle third. Rendered with the camera 200 m up, just above the tower tops (`node scripts/render-golden-gate.mjs archive/models/golden-gate archive/models/golden-gate/portrait-d --camera=1250,200,-130 --target=0,100,-40 --fov=20 --size=2400x2400`, then `python3 scripts/make-golden-gate-sprite.py archive/models/golden-gate/portrait-d 66 40 0.2 0.55 0.42 --out=golden-gate-portrait`). The render records where its eye level falls (7.4% of the sprite's height above the crop) and the stylesheet pins that row to the photo's horizon.
- `public/images/cruise-sky.jpg`, generated static fallback for loading and unavailable WebGL. Browsers with AVIF receive `cruise-sky.avif` (57,497 bytes, quality 70, encoded from the JPEG by Pillow) through `image-set()`, and the Worker’s Early Hint preloads that file with its type.
- Generation prompt: Photoreal 16:9 aviation sky at cruising altitude, porcelain blue sky, luminous white cumulus below a mid-low horizon, larger cloud banks lower right, warm upper-right sunlight, open left text space. No aircraft, buildings, text, logos, or window frames.

West -13639400, south 4558449.687, east -13615400, north 4574449.687, latitude 37.8514 to 37.9648. Produced by `python3 scripts/prepare-naip-layers.py /path/to/tile-cache marin`. No baked shade of its own: that would want a seventeenth texture unit, one past the WebGL2 minimum. Before it, the corridor's northern edge was the fine imagery's limit and the camera looked straight past it from progress 0.8; ray-marching the flight's own compositions put 51% of the visible ground outside every fine layer at progress 0.9 and 86% at 1.0, with the base map behind it all.

It is no longer sampled and no longer ships: its footprint sits entirely inside the tile pyramid, which schedules that ground at about one texel per pixel and, being the last layer, overrode it wherever tiles were resident. - The airport square and the runway box no longer ship as whole textures at runtime (`sfo-detail.webp` and `naip-runway.webp` stay in the repository as inputs to the tree and shadow scripts); their ground now streams as tiles, see "Streamed ground tiles" below.

Under the fallback sky non-water land retains the photograph's baked light; under the physical atmosphere it is treated as albedo lit by the same sun and sky. Low-elevation water receives restrained physical reflections from a generated tileable ripple normal at three scales, from metre ripples to kilometre wind streaks. Within about 900 m of the camera, two scales of the photographed asphalt colour and normal maps below add the grain that metre-scale orthoimagery cannot carry, fading out with distance.

## Corridor city massing

- `archive/scenery/bay-buildings.bin.gz` (229,536 buildings, 1,525,991 ring vertices, 4,757,242 bytes gzipped; `-mobile` 37,784 buildings, 1,105,533 bytes): every building footprint between the airport and the Golden Gate, from two public sources, packed by `python3 scripts/prepare-city-buildings.py fetch|fetch-sf|build --cache /path/to/cache`.
  - San Francisco (164,775 footprints): [DataSF Building Footprints](https://data.sf.gov/resource/ynuv-fyni.json), City and County of San Francisco, [ODC Public Domain Dedication and License](http://opendatacommons.org/licenses/pddl/1.0/). Footprints collapsed from a 2010 Pictometry model and split along parcel lines, with LiDAR-derived heights per footprint (`hgt_median_m`, the median first-return height above ground; peaks up to 35 % higher are honoured through `peak_1st_m` − `gnd_min_m`). Retrieved 2026-09-09 through the SODA API in pages of 40,000 rows.
  - Peninsula cities south of the county line at 37.7085° N (64,761 footprints): OpenStreetMap, 40 Overpass tiles covering south 37.60 / west -122.52 / north 37.715 / east -122.35, `way["building"]` and multipolygon relations with member geometry, retrieved 2026-09-09. **© OpenStreetMap contributors**, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), [attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The airport bbox already covered by `sfo-buildings.json` is excluded, as are underground and `building=no` features. Tagged `height` (metres or feet) is preferred, then `building:levels` × 3.3 m plus roof levels, otherwise a per-type estimate (7.5 m houses, 9 m generic residential, 13 m apartments, 14 m offices, 8–9 m warehouses, and so on) jittered ±12 % by a hash of the OSM id; untagged `building=yes` footprints over 900 m² are treated as warehouses. Nearly all peninsula buildings use such an estimate; only a few hundred carry height or level tags.
  - The packed binary is a derived database of both sources and stays under their licences; the script regenerates it.
- Processing: only outer rings are kept, simplified with a 1 m Douglas–Peucker tolerance and quantised to 0.5 m around each footprint's centre (byte offsets where a footprint fits in ±63 m, shorts otherwise); rings are written counter-clockwise, roofs that a fan from the first vertex cannot cover (concavities deeper than 0.5 m) carry ear-clipped triangles. Footprints under 30 m² and 12 m are dropped. Building kinds (residential, commercial, industrial, tower) drive the wall glazing; DataSF has no use tags, so there they follow height and footprint area. The file is stored gzip-compressed and inflated in the browser with `DecompressionStream`, since the host does not compress binary assets.
- Roof colours are sampled per building from the NAIP corridor layers at the footprint centre, so the walls (a lighter, desaturated derivative) agree with the photograph the roof itself is textured with.
- The phone subset keeps buildings at least 12 m tall or 500 m² in footprint.
- `lib/bay-city.ts` extrudes the file into one flat-shaded indexed mesh at runtime, in short slices between frames: a bottom ring sunk 1.5 m below the terrain and a roof ring at the highest ground point plus the height, with per-vertex wall colour, glazing amount in the alpha channel and a `lift` attribute that lets the city rise out of the imagery over about two seconds. Roofs are draped in the same imagery layers as the terrain through the shared Mercator projection; walls take the tint with floor banding and a darker street level. This is block massing from footprints, not a photogrammetric city.

## Baked shadows and sky visibility

- `archive/scenery/shade-south.webp`, `shade-north.webp` (4096², 3.91 m/px, 960,768 and 1,373,960 bytes) and `shade-sfo.webp` (4096², 2 m/px, 428,566 bytes), with 2048² `-mobile` versions: generated by `python3 scripts/prepare-bay-shadows.py --cache /path/to/city-cache`. Each imagery layer's height field is the terrain grid plus every building footprint (the corridor city and the airport set); the scene's fixed sun (26° elevation, west-south-west) is marched through it in image space with a 0.4 m bias and a 1.5 m soft edge, and eight horizon directions give a cosine-weighted sky-visibility term. Red is the direct-sun factor, green the sky visibility, blurred by 0.8 px and stored at WebP quality 60.
- `lib/bay-surface.ts` samples them through the same Mercator projection as the imagery, coarse to fine, and applies the sun factor to the directional light and the sky factor to image-based light (at half strength) on the terrain, the city's roofs and the tree canopies; walls only take the street-level shadow near the ground. A separate 3 m/px map for the city layer was baked and dropped: it would have pushed the terrain shader past the 16 texture units most GPUs expose.

## Cloud shadows

- A 256² tileable value noise (four octaves, seed 11, generated at load) drifts across the scene at about 6 m/s over a 3.5 km repeat. Where it exceeds a threshold it removes up to 45 % of the direct sun and 10 % of the sky light on terrain, city and trees; the aircraft samples the same field on the CPU at its own position so it darkens with the ground beneath it. The sky itself stays clear; the shadows read as thin cloud out of frame.

## Tree canopies

- `archive/scenery/bay-trees.bin.gz` (304,931 canopies, 1,737,852 bytes; `-mobile` 50,887 canopies, 325,686 bytes): `python3 scripts/prepare-bay-trees.py --cache /path/to/city-cache` classifies dark green pixels of the south and north NAIP layers that are not buildings (masks left by the shadow bake, grown by one pixel) and not water (terrain at sea level) as canopy, keeps 42 % of them at random (7 % for phones), and writes local metres (1 m), a diameter of 5–14 m biased by darkness, and the photograph's colour darkened toward green. Each record is 8 bytes.
- `lib/bay-trees.ts` draws them as one instanced octahedron per tree, twisted deterministically, coloured per instance and standing on the sampled terrain; canopies grow in over 1.5 s after loading. Lawns and roofs are excluded by the classifier's brightness threshold and the building mask, so parks, hillsides and street trees are covered but not every backyard tree.

## Golden Gate Bridge

- `lib/bay-bridge.ts` builds the bridge procedurally at load from its published dimensions: 227 m towers with two tapering legs and five portal struts, a 1,280 m main span between them and 343 m side spans, the two main cables (parabolic, sagging to 3 m above the roadway at midspan and meeting the tower tops), hanger ropes every 15.24 m, a 29 m deck with a 7.6 m stiffening truss cambered 4 m over the main span, concrete anchorage blocks with their pylons, the approach viaducts on steel bents down to the toll plaza and into the Marin headland, and the south tower's elliptical fender pier. About 8,700 triangles in five draw calls; International Orange (#C0362C) steel, a canvas-drawn roadway with six lanes and the yellow median, and the hanger ropes as translucent curtains that antialias by their pixel footprint so the veil still reads from kilometres away. The cables are inflated in the vertex shader so they never fall below about a pixel and a half wide.
- Position: the deck axis (`GOLDEN_GATE` in `lib/bay-surface.ts`) was fitted through the OpenStreetMap roadway nodes at both ends of the straight suspended section (US 101 ways 537838948 and 595194543, retrieved 2026-09-09; **© OpenStreetMap contributors**, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)), and the towers were placed on that axis where the NAIP photograph shows them, 1,280 m apart. The centreline through the curved approaches averages both carriageways' nodes. The bridge is not present in any of the height or footprint data, so it is a dimensioned model rather than a survey.
- The orthophoto shows the deck about 30 m east of its true position (relief displacement of a structure 70 m above the water) with the towers' shadows beside it. The terrain shader replaces that strip over the strait with water sampled 75–125 m further east, wandering along the deck so the copy does not streak; the photographed shadow stays as the bridge's shadow. The tree classifier now also excludes water (terrain at sea level), which had turned that shadow into a line of canopies.

## Easter eggs

- `lib/bay-easter-eggs.ts` reads the Konami code and a few typed words outside form fields, and clicks on the aircraft. Once the gear is up, a click waves the wings and three quick clicks, ↑↑↓↓←→←→BA or `roll` fly an aileron roll layered over the scroll-driven path (on the ground they only earn a hint), `gt350` or `shelby` toggle a chase car (`lib/bay-chase-car.ts`, a box-built Shelby GT350 that launches from the runway shoulder beside the aircraft and is dropped as the jet accelerates), and `karl` toggles Karl the Fog (`lib/bay-fog.ts`, seven translucent noise sheets pouring east through the Gate below the tower tops, lit by the scene's sun). The toggles are remembered in `localStorage`; the toasts come from the page. The car's paint is a constant in `lib/bay-chase-car.ts`.

## Livery

- `lib/bay-livery.ts` paints the site's own livery in the fragment shader from the model's own axes (nose −X, port +Z), so its edges stay crisp at any distance: a navy wave that starts under the wing box, sweeps up the aft fuselage and tail cone into the fin and curls over to the trailing edge, edged with the header's orange as a ribbon that widens through the fin; the same orange rings the engine inlets and edges the navy raked wingtips and the navy cowls. The edge is one side-elevation curve (a parabola on the fuselage handing over to a quadratic on the fin with matching slope), measured perpendicular to itself so the ribbon keeps its width, and cabin windows show through the paint. Lettering comes from a 2048² canvas drawn at load and redrawn once the page's fonts are ready: the header's plane glyph on an orange roundel on both sides of the fin (climbing toward the nose on each), `PERSONAL AIRSPACE` titles in Geist forward of the wing, the fictional registration N787HA aft, `henokabraham.com` in Geist Mono under the belly reading nose-left from below, and `HA` on both faces of each cowl. Text reads nose to tail on the port side and tail to nose on the starboard side, as on a real airframe.

## Visible thrust

- `lib/bay-thrust.ts` adds two effects driven by the flight's progress. Heat haze: a screen-space refraction in the post-processing chain (`HeatHazeEffect`, one pass after the ambient occlusion), shaped by two exhaust cones projected from the engines' core nozzles, 24 m long and widening to 3.4 m, whose turbulence streams aft at 9 m/s and is hidden wherever the scene depth is in front of the plume. The displacement scales with the engines' power setting (idle at the hold, spooling up at brake release, takeoff power through rotation, climb power once established) and with the inverse of the camera's distance, so the runway shimmers behind the aircraft at close range and the effect stays a few pixels from the chase camera. Wingtip vortices: helical vapour threads off both raked tips, two crossed noise-lit ribbons per tip in one mesh attached to the airframe, shown while the wing is loaded through rotation and the early climb and during the aileron roll and wing wave. Both are off for reduced-motion visitors.

## Streamed ground tiles

- `archive/tiles/` holds a six-level pyramid of 256 px ground tiles (0.30, 0.60, 1.20, 2.40, 4.80 and 9.60 Web Mercator metres per texel, about 0.24 to 7.6 real metres, each with a 4 px filtering border) over a 256 × 256 page grid (19.66 km, page grid origin west -13632500, south 4521200) covering the airport, the takeoff roll and the climb-out, plus `manifest.json`. Imagery: the two finest levels come from **San Mateo County GIS 2022 orthoimagery** (0.5 ft source, ArcGIS ImageServer `SanMateoCounty_Imagery2022`, reprojected to EPSG:3857 by the service, courtesy of the County of San Mateo; the county publishes no licence text for the service and its terms are to be confirmed with countygis@smcgov.org), with USGS NAIP (public domain) filling the county's coverage gaps; the four coarser levels come from NAIP outright, because the county service answers requests coarser than about a metre per pixel with near-white overviews. One per-channel linear fit against the NAIP corridor layer matches the whole set's tone (three quarters of the way to NAIP), so tiles feather into the 3.9 m layer without a seam.
- `scripts/prepare-bay-tiles.py schedule` replays the flight's own camera composition (a port of `sampleBayFlight` and `sampleBayCamera`) for 201 scroll buckets at four viewport aspects, marches a grid of view rays against the terrain grid and records, per bucket, the tiles needed for about one texel per screen pixel on a 1.5× laptop, with their ancestors, coarse first; nothing is scheduled beyond about 0.75 of the scroll, where the ground is far enough for the corridor layers. Two sets sit outside the buckets: the floor, 164 tiles across the two coarsest levels, retained on desktop when capacity allows so the ground never falls back to the 10 m base map (the corridor layer's feathered southern edge runs through the airport), and the airport square at 2.4 m with its ancestors, wanted while the scroll is below 0.46. `build --cache` fetches the imagery in 15 × 15-tile blocks and cuts the tiles.
- `lib/bay-tiles.ts` chooses an atlas from the GPU texture limit: up to 24 × 24 slots / 6336² px on desktop (153.1 MiB RGBA8), or 15 × 15 / 3960² px on phones (59.8 MiB). Phones skip level 0 and retain only the 64-tile coarsest floor; 4096-limit desktops also use that floor. Smaller limits reduce detail and may omit the permanent floor to preserve current coverage. Current-bucket tiles are requested before the floor and optional airport/lookahead sets. Visible frames upload at most four tiles (two on phones) and maintain the ancestor page table incrementally per placement and eviction, uploading its 349,524-byte mip arrays once per batch. Aborted requests cannot publish stale decodes; transient failures retry after two seconds. Priming waits for its own bucket, independently of table changes, for at most twelve seconds, and settles on disposal. The shaders use two samplers for the page table and mip-less atlas, blend adjacent pyramid levels, and hand over to whole-image layers at distance. Tile crop, image decoding orientation and atlas UV mapping are unchanged.
- The manifest contains exactly **1,671 unique tiles and 16,150,728 compressed bytes** (about 16 MB). The scenery check verifies every listed tile exists and reconciles the byte total; per-visit transfers vary with device detail, cache and scrolling/revisits. No tile imagery or schedule was regenerated for these runtime fixes.

## Freeway traffic

- `archive/scenery/bay-roads.json` (53,100 bytes): 405 one-way carriageways of US 101, I-380 and I-280 between the airport and San Bruno Mountain (95 km, motorways and their links, simplified to 1 m, with lane counts), packed by `python3 scripts/prepare-bay-traffic.py fetch|build --cache /path/to/cache` from OpenStreetMap (`highway=motorway|motorway_link` within south 37.598 / west -122.462 / north 37.662 / east -122.381, retrieved 2026-09-09). **© OpenStreetMap contributors**, [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), [attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); a derived database that stays under that licence.
- `lib/bay-traffic.ts` fills every lane with box vehicles (a body with a set-back cabin, about one per 78 m of lane on the motorways and 150 m on the links, 11 % of them trucks, in a weighted paint mix) and drives them along the polylines at lane-dependent speeds in one instanced draw call; the matrices are rewritten each frame on the CPU. Elevations come from the terrain grid, so the freeways sit on the ground rather than on their real viaducts. Loaded with the corridor layers after the first frame; phones get under half the density; reduced motion freezes it.

## Open-source atmosphere and rendering update

`archive/scenery/atmosphere/{transmittance,scattering,irradiance}.exr` are unchanged reference lookup textures from Takram's MIT-licensed three-geospatial project, fetched at low priority while the GPU generates the same tables, and used when they arrive first; revision `eac103980f20c0956f2d3215833e73514be08462`. Together they contain 4,124,561 bytes. They are rendered by `@takram/three-atmosphere` 0.19.1, with Bruneton's BSD-licensed scattering functions. They describe atmospheric light transport, not geographic imagery. The former cloud sprites are retained as source assets but no longer rendered in the clear-sky sequence.
