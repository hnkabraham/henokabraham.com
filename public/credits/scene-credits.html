The world behind the flight.

A cinematic, scroll-controlled departure from San Francisco, built with Three.js. The aircraft, satellite imagery, terrain and lighting are credited below.

Boeing 787-9

Boeing 787-9 by Nobilis2, used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. The optimized model was sourced through God’s Eye View’s credited model collection. That derivative simplifies geometry and materials and uses 256px WebP textures. This site adds folding landing gear, elastic wing flex, fan movement and physical paint and metal shading. These changes are not endorsed by the original author.

San Francisco Bay imagery

Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2019), processed by ESA. ESA’s San Francisco Bay image, acquired January 25, 2019. Cropped and resized to 4096px and 2048px textures. Both derivatives remain licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO.

Elevation and registration

Mapzen terrain tiles; United States 3DEP and global terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey; ocean terrain data from NOAA. Full terrain data attribution. Ninety Terrarium tiles at zoom 12 (columns 652–660, rows 1579–1588) were resampled into a 1025 × 1025 grid, with nonpositive elevations clamped to water level and the runway apron flattened.

The satellite photograph was approximately aligned to USGS reference imagery. Registration metadata and landmark coordinates. The route, runway detailing and aircraft motion are a cinematic reconstruction; the wider city uses satellite imagery over terrain; airport buildings use simplified real footprints.

SFO aerial and airport buildings

Close aerial imagery: USGS, USDA, The National Map: Orthoimagery. Public-domain NAIP imagery, acquired May 18, 2022, from a 0.6-meter source and resampled for this scene.

Building footprints: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The separately licensed footprint dataset retains source identifiers. Tagged heights are used where available; missing heights are estimated for the scene. Buildings are simplified extrusions, and the imagery and footprints come from different dates.

Runway pavement: Asphalt 02 by Rob Tuytel, Poly Haven, CC0. Diffuse, normal and roughness maps create the surface detail.

Sky, clouds and sound

Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky) by Greg Zaal and Jarod Guest, CC0, supplies daylight reflections. The sky and distance haze use Takram’s atmosphere renderer, based on Eric Bruneton’s precomputed atmospheric scattering. The lookup textures are bundled with this site. N8AO adds contact shadows, and pmndrs postprocessing supplies HDR composition, restrained bloom and AgX tone mapping. Open-source license notices. A simpler Three.js sky is retained as a compatibility fallback. The static fallback sky was generated for this portfolio. Optional jet and wind ambience is synthesized locally with Web Audio and starts only when enabled.
