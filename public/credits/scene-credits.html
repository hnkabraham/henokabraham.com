The world behind the flight.

A cinematic, scroll-controlled departure from San Francisco, built with Three.js. The aircraft, satellite imagery, terrain and lighting are credited below.

Boeing 787-9

Boeing 787-9 by Nobilis2, used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. The optimized model was sourced through God’s Eye View’s credited model collection. That derivative simplifies geometry and materials and uses 256px WebP textures. This site adds retractable landing gear, fan movement and physical daylight shading. These changes are not endorsed by the original author.

San Francisco Bay imagery

Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2019), processed by ESA. ESA’s San Francisco Bay image, acquired January 25, 2019. Cropped and resized to 4096px and 2048px textures. Both derivatives remain licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO.

Elevation and registration

Mapzen terrain tiles; United States 3DEP and global terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey; ocean terrain data from NOAA. Full terrain data attribution. Four Terrarium tiles at zoom 10 (163–164, 395–396) were resampled into a 257 × 257 grid, with nonpositive elevations clamped to water level and the runway apron flattened.

The satellite photograph was approximately aligned to USGS reference imagery. Registration metadata and landmark coordinates. The route, runway detailing and aircraft motion are a cinematic reconstruction; the city uses satellite imagery over terrain rather than a building-by-building city model.

Sky, clouds and sound

Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky) by Greg Zaal and Jarod Guest, CC0, supplies daylight reflections. Atmospheric sky uses Three.js’s Sky shader. Cloud sprites and the static fallback sky were generated for this portfolio. Optional jet and wind ambience is synthesized locally with Web Audio and starts only when enabled.
