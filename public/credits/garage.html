Garage model credits

The 2017 Shelby GT350 (non-R) in the Garage section represents my own car, adapted from the R-trim 2016 Ford Mustang Shelby GT350R Sketchfab model by Ddiaz Design, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

The 550 separate mesh parts in the original file were merged into one primitive per material, the body paint was repainted to match my own car — gray, with blue racing stripes, in place of the model's default scheme — the textures’ vertical coordinate was turned over from USD’s convention to glTF’s, which the conversion had first missed, so the badges, tires, interior and trim read as the original artist painted them, the GT350R’s red Shelby snake badges on the grille and trunk were recolored the base GT350’s polished alloy, the large rear wing and its supports were removed, the GT350R’s carbon wheel faces were replaced with the base GT350’s own ten-spoke wheel, which I modeled from measurements of the part, the roof panel — whose own UV layout painted almost the entire panel in the stripe color rather than tracking the body's gray-with-stripes pattern — was given new texture coordinates so it carries the same stripes as the hood and trunk, textures were re-encoded to WebP, and the result was Draco-compressed for the web. This derivative remains CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial use, share-alike, with this attribution.

The remaining body geometry, and the wheels’ tires, rims and brakes, still come from the GT350R source, so this is a cosmetic approximation of my non-R GT350, not a scan of mine. A small lip spoiler is not separately modeled here — a nice enough stand-in until I get around to scanning the actual one. Its reflections are captured from the park scene around it.

Dolores Park scene

The Garage shows the car parked in an interactive three.js reconstruction of 20th Street at the top of Mission Dolores Park, San Francisco; the picture shown while it loads is rendered from the same scene (its source is scripts/dolores-park/ in this site’s repository). The default view’s framing was matched by eye to a street-level view from 20th Street; no Street View, satellite-map or other proprietary imagery was used to make it. The ground, buildings and tree positions come from public data. The foreground tree, palms, benches and street detail are modeled, and the row of painted Victorians across the street is invented rather than a copy of the real houses, so it is a reconstruction, not a photograph. Nearby streets, houses and trees are real geometry; the distant park, city and sky are a backdrop baked from the same scene. Because the GT350 above is in it, the rendered scene and its picture are likewise CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the park scene’s own models (dolores-park.glb, dolores-park-phone.glb) don’t include the car, and carry the licences of the sources below.
