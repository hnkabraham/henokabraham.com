# Rendering decisions

The flight uses Three.js with Takram's atmosphere, N8AO and pmndrs postprocessing. These improve the existing aircraft and Bay scenery while keeping native scrolling, mobile layouts, local assets and the current deployment. The wider city is satellite imagery on elevation geometry with every OpenStreetMap footprint in the corridor extruded on top; the airport uses its own footprint set. Rendering changes do not turn those assets into photogrammetry.

## Pipeline and compatibility

1. Render PBR aircraft, real terrain, asphalt and building geometry to a half-float target. The meshes are lit in the atmosphere's own relative-luminance units: the directional sun takes its colour from the transmittance table at the aircraft's position (`getSunLightColor`), and image-based diffuse and specular light comes from a 128² (64² on phones) cubemap of Takram's `SkyMaterial` rendered at the camera’s scene position and prefiltered with PMREM, refreshed after 150 m of camera movement. The sun disc is excluded from that cubemap because the directional light already provides it. Terrain imagery is treated as albedo under this light; only under the fallback sky does it keep its baked exposure.
2. Apply N8AO from the actual scene depth, preserving wing displacement. N8AO 2.0.1's internal beauty target is explicitly changed from unsigned byte to half float so highlights are not clipped before composition.
3. Apply atmospheric transmittance and inscattering. Post-process sun/sky lighting remains disabled because the materials already receive PBR light. The Bruneton lookup tables come from whichever source lands first: a low-priority, abortable download of the shipped EXR tables (4 MB), or the library's `PrecomputedTexturesGenerator` (half-float, without the higher-order scattering table; a few dozen small passes spread over idle callbacks, roughly half a second of GPU time on a laptop), which starts only when the download's arrival rate at 400 ms projects it past 900 ms, or unconditionally at 900 ms. Fast connections never generate; slow ones use the generated tables and abort the download, so the opening no longer waits on that transfer. Atmospheric sky replaces the Three.js fallback sky only after the tables exist. Exponential fog is removed at the same time to prevent applying haze twice.
4. Add restrained HDR bloom, then AgX tone mapping at exposure 2.1 (so a sunlit white surface sits near the start of the AgX shoulder while the zenith stays a deep blue), a 12% saturation recovery for the sky and land that AgX compresses, and dithering. Tone mapping and gamma conversion are applied once.

Before the canvas fades in, every program is compiled asynchronously. An offscreen/hidden opening defers drawing until it becomes visible. Cleanup handles exist before any asset awaits, priming settles on disposal, and scene traversal releases InstancedMesh instance buffers in addition to geometry, materials and textures.

Phone settings use lower AO resolution, fewer bloom levels, a 512² terrain grid and a smaller sky cubemap; multisampling and a bounded pixel budget keep high-DPI render targets under control. Reduced-motion behavior and offscreen/hidden-tab suspension are preserved. Missing atmospheric textures retain the simpler sky; missing floating-point framebuffer support uses the direct renderer. Every composer target, lookup texture and N8AO fullscreen wrapper is disposed on unmount.

## Motion and the city

- Scroll progress is followed with a short lag and capped at 0.22 of the route per second, so a flick through the page still flies for a few seconds instead of teleporting the aircraft. Ground detail, runway edge lights and the sky refresh cannot strobe past the camera, which is what fast scrolling previously produced as brief light flashes.
- The sky cubemap is refreshed initially and after the camera has moved at least 150 m in scene space (aircraft position plus camera offset). Stationary frames never rebuild it, regardless of elapsed time. The sun colour still follows the atmosphere origin each draw.
- The shadow camera reaches 8 km along the sun, and the aircraft's shadow fades between 900 m and 2,300 m of altitude instead of cutting off when the ground left the old 1.2 km frustum. The depth bias scales with the new range.
- The reading-area scrim cross-fades between its preflight and in-flight gradients rather than switching.
- The corridor city is one indexed, flat-shaded draw call (about 4.1 million triangles on desktop) built after the first frame and lit like the terrain, including N8AO between neighbours and the aircraft's shadow on roofs near the airport. Its own shadows and the sky it hides from the streets come from the baked maps described in ASSETS.md, not the shadow map, which only covers the aircraft.
- After the first frame the page measures 45 frame times. A desktop with a median under 21 ms and 8192-px textures gets the full city, the full canopy set and, once its export exists, the 1.5 m city imagery; anything slower, and every phone, gets the phone-sized city and canopy instead. After 8 s without a completed measurement (a tab hidden through the window), the lazy loads start on the next visible frame: desktops whose texture limit reaches 8192 px take the full variants, as a measured desktop would, and everything else takes the phone-sized ones.
- The wide satellite image ships as 2048² in the opening set on every device, with a 4096² version requested after the first frame on desktop and swapped into the terrain material. It is what shows wherever no corridor layer or streamed tile covers the ground, which ray-marching the flight's own compositions puts at 86% of the visible ground by the end of the climb, so 23 m/px magnified there into smeared shadow over the Marin hills. The opening download is unchanged.
- The Golden Gate Bridge is a procedural model in the deck frame fitted from OpenStreetMap (ASSETS.md), built after the first frame with no download, in five draw calls under the same cloud shadows as the ground. The photographed deck beneath it is masked out of the imagery in the terrain shader; the flight passes 5–7 km east of it during the Bay chapter, where the towers, cables and hanger veil read at that distance.
- The airfield's lights are about 5,000 instanced spheres in five draw calls (one per colour) plus the flashers, whose instance colours are rewritten each frame; the parked fleet is two instanced draw calls. The airfield loads with the first frame from a 62 KB JSON file and costs no texture units.
- Easter eggs (wing wave, aileron roll, chase car, fog) are layered over the scroll-driven flight: the roll adds a smoothly started 360° bank to the path's own attitude for 3.6 s without touching scroll progress, and the props are built only when first switched on. Reduced-motion visitors get no stunts.
- The livery is analytic: the navy wave, ribbon, cowls and wingtips are signed-distance shapes in model space antialiased with `fwidth`, and only the lettering samples a texture (one 2048² atlas, one extra sampler on the skin materials), so the paint reads sharp from the runway camera to the chase camera without a large decal texture.
- Thrust: an `EffectPass` between N8AO and the aerial perspective carries the engines' heat haze, a `mainUv` distortion computed per pixel from two exhaust segments in view space (closest point between the view ray and each segment, depth-occluded with `readDepth`/`getViewZ`), so it costs no extra draw calls or render targets. The wingtip vapour is one transparent mesh drawn after the opaque scene. Note that a single NaN pixel from a transparent shader is spread by the bloom's mip chain into a large black square, so ribbon shaders guard every `pow` base.
- The climb-out has its own 1.46 m imagery layer in the desktop-only sampler slot, fading in after the first frame, and one instanced draw call of freeway traffic whose instance matrices are recomputed on the CPU each frame (a few thousand vehicles, about a millisecond).
- The airport and climb-out ground is a streamed virtual texture (`lib/bay-tiles.ts`): the fragment shader derives a pyramid level from the screen-space footprint of a level-0 texel (max axis, so grazing views never alias), reads a nearest-filtered page table at that mip with `textureLod`, samples the resident tile inside its 4 px border in a linear-filtered, mip-less atlas, blends the two nearest levels, and fades to the whole-texture layers past the coarsest level where the atlas could no longer minify. Decoded tiles queue for visible-frame `copyTextureToTexture` uploads, capped at four per frame on desktop and two on phones. Fetch/decode plus queued bitmap counts are bounded by concurrency. The page-table mip chain (349,524 bytes) is maintained incrementally: a placement claims only the cells of its footprint that pointed at a coarser ancestor, an eviction hands its cells to the nearest ancestor still resident, and the chain uploads once per batch; publication precedes drawing. The atlas stays mip-less and uses explicit level-zero sampling. Whole-image/shade lookups use explicit gradients, and bridge-replacement gradients are computed before its varying branch. Because the camera path is fixed, residency is scheduled offline rather than through a feedback pass.
- Display grade: AgX is a strongly desaturating curve. Measured across six scroll positions it left the mid-climb at 6% mean saturation and the last frame at 74 levels between the 5th and 95th luminance percentiles. `GRADE` in `lib/bay-rendering.ts` corrects it after tone mapping: saturation 0.252 in one `HueSaturationEffect` (the operator is nonlinear in its parameter, so this is the single value equal to the old 0.12 followed by the 0.15 measurement settled on), and a contrast of 0.04 with a brightness of 0.012. postprocessing's contrast pivots at 0.5 in linear, which sits well above this scene's median, so the brightness re-pivots it onto the median: without it the whole image dropped 30 levels. Result across the six frames: saturation up about a fifth in relative terms, medians within 2 levels, no clipping. Plain saturation was chosen over vibrance because vibrance amplifies the chroma of near-neutral pixels, and it turned the mid-climb's concrete rooftops magenta.
- The grade cannot do more than that. The mid-climb reads grey because its subject is grey — warehouse roofs and untextured city massing — and the last quarter is flat because 4 km of air over 15 to 25 km of ground genuinely veils it. Both are content and atmosphere problems, not curve problems.
- `renderer.toneMappingExposure` is live on the atmosphere path even though the renderer's own tone mapping is off: postprocessing injects Three's tone-mapping chunk, and its `AgXToneMapping` does `color *= toneMappingExposure`. Removing the assignment halves the image. `scripts/check-bay-rendering.mjs` asserts both the shader term and the scene's assignment.
- Texture units: the terrain material uses all 16 fragment samplers of the WebGL2 minimum, which is what this machine's ANGLE/Metal driver reports (base map, three whole-texture imagery layers, the tile page table and atlas, three shade maps, cloud noise, pavement colour and normal, water normal, shadow map, environment). It is a hard ceiling, not a guideline: adding a seventeenth fails to link with `FRAGMENT shader texture image units count exceeds MAX_TEXTURE_IMAGE_UNITS(16)` and the terrain does not draw at all. The north-bay layer got its unit by retiring the climb-out layer, whose footprint the tile pyramid already covers (see ASSETS.md); anything further needs another one freed first.

## Residency and startup

The atlas edge is capped by `renderer.capabilities.maxTextureSize`. Desktop requests 24 × 24 slots (6336² px, 153.1 MiB RGBA8); phones request 15 × 15 (3960² px, 59.8 MiB), safely within a 4096-px limit. These are atlas-only allocations, excluding other textures and render targets. Desktop keeps the manifest's 164-tile floor where it fits. Phones and 4096-limit desktops keep only the 64 coarsest tiles. A 2048 limit uses 7 × 7 slots, no permanent floor, and a coarser minimum level chosen by replaying every bucket; the whole-image layers cover distant gaps. An insufficient limit fails to the existing scene fallback rather than allocating an oversized atlas.

Current-bucket tiles precede the floor, the airport square and directional lookahead (ten ahead, two behind), with four spare slots. The runtime checks the current bucket plus floor against capacity across the complete manifest; only optional prefetch coverage is trimmed. Normal desktop/phone settings keep all bucket tiles at their configured minimum levels (0/1). Request identity survives fetch and bitmap decode, aborted completions close their images, and only a matching request may clear its pending entry. Placement is idempotent; eviction checks slot ownership. Failures retry after two seconds while active, including at stationary progress.

Priming waits for the opening bucket's own coverage, independently of dirty page-table state, with a twelve-second cap. Missing tiles do not count as ready. Priming settles immediately if already covered and settles on disposal or replacement; startup uses the same frame upload budget. Tile orientation and cropping are unchanged. Offscreen/hidden scenes skip animation updates, scheduling and uploads; late imagery, shade maps, city, trees and procedural props invalidate reduced-motion rendering, whose reveals settle immediately.

The lazy imagery and shade layers (4096² and 6144² on desktop) are fetched and decoded with `createImageBitmap`, off the main thread, and uploaded with `renderer.initTexture` one texture per visible frame from a queue, so the city, trees and imagery arriving together cannot stall a scroll with a single frame of decodes and uploads; the decoded bitmaps are closed after upload. Whole-image imagery and shade bitmaps use `imageOrientation: 'flipY'` at decode time to preserve TextureLoader's north-up UV orientation; Three.js ignores `Texture.flipY` for ImageBitmap uploads. Elevation readback and the tile-atlas copy retain their separate orientation conventions. Every image fetch (elevation grid, tiles, lazy layers) reads the body as an ArrayBuffer and wraps it in a Blob for decoding: Chromium rejects `Response.blob()` on slow or preload-matched bodies with a bare "Failed to fetch", which on an 8 Mbps connection took the whole opening down to the fallback. The document preloads the opening's model, elevation grid, tile manifest, airport JSON and the runway and wide-area textures at low fetch priority behind a `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` media query, so those requests start with the HTML rather than after the module chain has evaluated without delaying the scripts; the Worker also announces the tile manifest as a `Link: rel=preload` header for Cloudflare's Early Hints (a Link header cannot carry a low priority, so larger assets stay out of it).

The existing offline schedule is unchanged. Its Python camera port and distance/grazing-angle footprint remain approximations; runtime residency validation does not prove per-pixel footprint coverage at every viewport or DPR. Replacing that estimator is separate from these runtime fixes.

## Validation

`scripts/check-bay-rendering.mjs` decodes all three actual EXR lookup textures (the fallback set), verifies dimensions and finite half-float samples, checks 1,001 floating-origin transforms and SFO coordinates, samples the sun colour the scene lights with from the transmittance table, checks that the renderer races the GPU generator against a low-priority abortable download of the shipped tables, and exercises the installed atmosphere/postprocessing shader assembler. The flight checks also validate the lossless WebP elevation grid's 1025² header. The existing flight checks validate camera framing and route continuity; scenery checks validate geometry and material/depth shader integration. The scenery check now verifies all 1,671 shipped tile files/bytes, replays all 201 buckets in both directions for desktop/mobile and 4096/2048 limits, compares 400 incremental page-table placements and evictions against full rebuilds, and runs controlled fetch/decode race, retry, batching and priming checks without network or GPU access. Rendering checks execute the actual cleanup body at five startup stages and the animation body under hidden/reduced-motion conditions, including instance disposal, render invalidation and the visible-frame gating of tile and texture uploads, and check the sky movement threshold. Flight checks cover project budgets, every project/chapter link round trip, replace-only history, annotation intervals and logbook provenance constraints. TypeScript and the production build verify the installed dependency combination. These checks do not constitute browser visual or device-performance QA.

The tangent-plane scenery introduces under 55 meters of ellipsoid-height difference at the farthest point of this cinematic route. It is not a navigational simulation.

## Bundled atmosphere data

The three EXRs are downloaded at low priority alongside GPU generation and used when they arrive first; they are copied unchanged from the asset revision referenced by `@takram/three-atmosphere` 0.19.1: [`eac103980f20c0956f2d3215833e73514be08462`](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial/tree/eac103980f20c0956f2d3215833e73514be08462/packages/atmosphere/assets). Total compressed size: 4,124,561 bytes. Scattering is a 256 × 128 × 32 lookup volume stored as a 256 × 4096 EXR; transmittance is 256 × 64, irradiance 64 × 16.

N8AO's distributed LICENSE and README state CC0 1.0, while its npm metadata says ISC; the distributed license is reproduced unchanged. Full notices for these projects and Three.js/postprocessing are included in `public/credits/rendering-licenses.txt` and linked from the scene credits.
